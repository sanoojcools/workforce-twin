The customer's day

Meera has 48 hours before the CEO announces five days in the office.

Meera is CHRO of a 5,000-person IT services company in Bengaluru (a composite, not a real person). Today her people work two set days in the office. The CEO wants five, starting next quarter. The draft email is on her screen.

1

She pastes the draft.

2

The twin builds her workforce and asks it.

5,000 people, split into 24 groups by gender, level, commute and caring duties, in her real headcount mix. Two people from every group read the email and say what they would actually do. Their answers are weighted back up to 5,000.

Each square is one group, sized by headcount. Colour shows how much the mandate raises that group's chance of leaving.

She listens.

Can she trust it?

We ran it on three real policies with published results, and we publish the miss.

The twin was shown each company, the status quo and the announcement. It was never shown the outcome. Each policy was run three times with fresh voices.

Who it is for

CHRO

Walks into the CEO conversation with a number, the groups at risk and a better version of the policy, not a gut feel.

CEO and CFO

Sees regrettable attrition as a cost before the decision, alongside the real-estate savings.

HR business partners

Know which teams to sit with first, and what those people will say, on announcement day.

Employees

Their likely reactions shape the policy before it ships, instead of in an exit interview after.

Limits
  • Three backtests is a small sample. The say-do fix was written after seeing the return-to-office miss, so that result is in-sample.
  • The studies measure different things over different windows (a six-month trial, an event study of turnover, a six-month pilot). The twin forecasts a 12-month change, so the comparison is approximate.
  • The models may have read these studies. The twin is never told the outcome, but contamination cannot be ruled out; that is why the forward case and the human check matter.
  • The workforce is synthetic. A real deployment would build the 24 groups from the company's HRIS, never from individuals' records, and would keep the output at group level.
